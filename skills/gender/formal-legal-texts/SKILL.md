## Purpose

Apply inclusive language to contracts, collective agreements, legislation, and
official policies **without changing meaning, application, scope, or value** —
the non-negotiable constraint for this register.

## When to use

Any text with legal effect or binding interpretation: laws, decrees, CBAs,
HR policies, insurance terms, T&Cs, official forms.

## Rules

**R1 — Supremacy of scope.** If a candidate rewrite could plausibly alter who is
covered, what is owed, when, or how much — reject it. Prefer the heavier but
unambiguous construction.

**R2 — Repeat the noun; do not create pronoun ambiguity.**
- "The Employer may authorize the employee to work his normal work day" →
  "…to work **the employee's** normal work day". Here "their" would be ambiguous
  (employer vs employee) — do not use it.
- When a sentence has two person-subjects, repeat nouns throughout (CAPE).

**R3 — Pluralisation is dangerous in obligations.**
- a) "Each employee is expected to organize his hours of work…" (individual duty)
- b) "Employees are expected to organize their hours of work…" (may read as collective)
If individuality matters, keep singular subject + singular they, or repeat the noun.
Never pluralise to fix pronouns where duty allocation is load-bearing.

**R4 — Passive voice only when agency is irrelevant.**
"The grievor must have reliable evidence of the situation he is describing" →
"…of the situation being described." Acceptable because the actor is obvious;
unacceptable where responsibility assignment matters.

**R5 — Remove legacy interpretive clauses carefully.**
Delete "the masculine gender includes the feminine gender" clauses **only** as part
of a competent amendment process; if a replacement interpretive sentence is needed,
it must state gender-neutrality explicitly and be flagged for human/legal review.
Never silently delete operative text.

**R6 — Statutory drafting order.** Where legislation historically defaulted to
male pronouns, EIGE sanctions deliberate reversal ("her/his … she/he") to counter
entrenched male-default reading. Use only when the client accepts the convention;
otherwise use neutral constructions.

**R7 — Entitlements terminology.** Some terms are defined legal entitlements:
"maternity benefits" refers to specific EI/QPIP-type programs. Do not rename
statutory benefit labels; if modernisation is requested, use dual forms like
"pregnancy/maternity" per program rules and flag for counsel.

**R8 — Forms attached to legal processes:** add non-binary option (never "Other");
optional Pronouns field; ensure every question remains answerable by all family
compositions (parent-1/parent-2 instead of mother/father unless legally required).

**R9 — Update discipline.** When amending existing instruments: change nothing
beyond agreed clauses; produce a redline-style change log mapping each edit to its
rule; mark any edit with interpretive risk as `requires-human-review`.

## Worked example (EIGE-2019 legal text)

Original:
> Every person has the right to choose his employer and to enter into work of his
> own volition, assuming he fulfils the necessary qualification criteria…

Revised (deliberate reversal convention):
> Every person has the right to choose her/his employer and to enter into work of
> her/his own volition, assuming she/he fulfils the necessary qualification criteria…

Neutral alternative (preferred by default):
> Every person has the right to choose their employer and to enter into work of
> their own volition, assuming they fulfil the necessary qualification criteria…

## Guardrails

- G1 overrides everything in this skill. When in doubt → neutral construction +
  `requires-human-review` flag.
- This skill never provides legal advice; it flags interpretive risk only.

## Uncertainty

Any doubt about interpretive effect ⇒ output both the conservative revision and a
note describing the risk; never choose silently between readings.
