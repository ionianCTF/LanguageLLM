## Purpose

Describe age and age groups precisely and without stereotype, infantilisation, or
disparagement.

## When to use

Any content mentioning age, generations, or age groups.

## Rules

**R1 — Prefer precise descriptors over vague/informal labels.** Name the actual
band: "people in their sixties", "octogenarians", "teenagers", "first-year
students".

**R2 — Terminology table (avoid → use):**

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| OAPs, the elderly, the aged, seniors | older adults, older people, people in their sixties/seventies, octogenarians etc. |
| senile | person with dementia |
| the youth (as monolith) | young people, people aged 18–24 (be specific) |
| geriatric (for people generally) | older people ("geriatric" = medical specialty context only) |

**R3 — No stereotyped trait assignment.** Do not code younger people as
irresponsible/tech-native-by-default or older people as infirm, out-of-touch, or
childlike. Describe behaviour, not age-derived expectations.

**R4 — Age descriptors are often gendered.** Follow individuals' self-description:
"female adolescent", "young man", "gender-queer child", "gender-fluid woman"
(route gender terms through lgbtqia-language / pronoun-strategy).

**R5 — Intersectionality.** Older women — particularly with migrant background —
face compounding disadvantages in e.g. energy poverty (EIGE-2024). Don't let
single-axis age framing erase this.

## Guardrails

- G3: mention age only when relevant.
- G10: no judgemental comparatives about age groups ("surprisingly competent for
  her age" → describe competence directly).

## Uncertainty

Age-band conventions vary by country/context (e.g. "senior citizen" is standard in
some jurisdictions' benefits language). Defer to local official terminology where
one exists; flag it.
