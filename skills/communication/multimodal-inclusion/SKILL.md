## Purpose

Extend inclusive-language principles beyond prose into visuals, video, audio,
social posts, and live events.

## When to use

Any task producing or reviewing non-textual or mixed-media communication.

## Rules

### Images & illustrations
**R1 — Role diversity.** Show women and men (and gender-diverse people where
contextually appropriate) in a variety of roles; reverse traditional norms
deliberately (men as parents, women as leaders). Never reproduce the
doctors-male/nurses-female default pattern.
**R2 — Body position & activity audit.** Ask: Are men dominating (leading the
meeting) while women support (passive participants)? Are roles equally
attributable? Fix composition, not just headcount.
**R3 — Representation breadth.** Diverse racial/ethnic backgrounds, ages, body
shapes, abilities; disabled people shown in roles beyond patient/dependent;
LGBTQ+-inclusive imagery (same-sex couples/families) where relevant.
**R4 — Colour discipline.** No pink-for-women/blue-for-men shorthand; audit the
palette for feminised/masculinised topic coding (nature=feminine, defence=
masculine are stereotypes to break).
**R5 — Emoji.** Use current emoji sets with diverse professions/genders; avoid
sets casting men active/women decorative; keep software current for widest options.

### Video
**R6 — Production diversity:** gender balance among writers/directors/editors.
**R7 — Content checks:** broader understanding of gender incl. non-binary;
behaviours not limited by stereotype; equal screen time; captions/subtitles use
inclusive language; voice-over script free of bias; music not stereotype-coded.
**R8 — Script audit questions:** traditional roles? modern family diversity?
who provides knowledge/expertise? who expresses concern/care?

### Voice-over
**R9 — Voice casting must not code topics by gender** (feminine voice for family
life, masculine for technology). Single voice → interrogate what the gender choice
signals; prefer variety across a campaign.

### Social media
**R10 — Post text follows all Tier 2/3 skills** (no "guys", no gendered defaults).
**R11 — Hashtags:** capitalise each word (#ThisIsGreat) for screen-reader/
voice-assistant accessibility; audit hashtags for stereotype reinforcement.
**R12 — Sensitivity review:** run internal review with a diverse panel; monitor
audience feedback as signal.

### Events
**R13 — Panels/moderators:** gender-balanced compositions; moderators from
diverse backgrounds who actively encourage floor participation.
**R14 — Q&A language:** gender-neutral pointers ("the person in the green shirt").
**R15 — Registration forms:** Pronouns field (optional); non-binary option (never
"Other"); dietary restrictions; disability accommodations; inclusivity disclaimer
welcoming all, zero-tolerance for harassment.
**R16 — Venue/design:** accessibility (ramp access), gender-neutral toilets plus
male/female, baby-changing for all parents, sign-language interpreters,
accessible programmes (visually impaired).

## Output format for reviews

Per asset: finding, category (role/composition/colour/audio/text/accessibility),
severity (reuse global scale), concrete fix.

## Guardrails

- G3 applies visually too: diverse representation ≠ token checklist; represent
  what the content's audience/context actually involves.
- Do not demand impossible stock: if suitable inclusive assets don't exist,
  recommend commissioning/adjusting rather than accepting stereotype defaults.

## Uncertainty

Cultural context changes what reads as stereotyped (colour codes differ by
market). State assumed cultural context when reviewing.
